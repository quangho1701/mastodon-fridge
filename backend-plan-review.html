Backend Plan — Senior Engineering Review

Independent review of backend-high-level-plan.html from the perspective of a senior backend engineer. The plan is directionally sound for a 50-user MVP, but several decisions need sharpening before Phase 0 starts — especially around the dev-identity approach, RLS posture, the gallery moderation gap, and a handful of missing tables.

Reviewer: senior backend perspective Project: Mastodon Fridge (Expo + Supabase) Target: ~50 PFW users Status: pre-implementation

Bottom Line

Choosing Supabase over a custom Express/Fastify stack is the right call for this scale, and the principle of "delay auth in the UX, not in the schema" is exactly correct. However, the plan is light on operational specifics that will bite the project once real testing starts: how the dev identity actually authenticates, where secrets live, how RLS is exercised during development, and how galleries handle user-generated content. There are also three or four tables and a few fields the schema needs that aren't called out yet.

I'd estimate this plan is ~75% ready. The remaining 25% is the difference between a smooth Phase 0 and discovering at Phase 6 that auth migration is painful because RLS was never enabled and dev data has no real owner.

Keep as-is

  • Supabase over custom backend
  • Phased delivery (manual events before AI)
  • Future-safe ownership fields
  • Storing media paths, not blobs, in Postgres
  • Skipping official PFW SSO for MVP

Tighten

  • Dev-identity authorization mechanics
  • RLS on from day 1 with permissive dev policies
  • Storage path conventions and bucket policies
  • Secret management for Anthropic + OAuth keys
  • Indexes, timestamps, soft deletes

Rework or add

  • Gallery moderation cannot be deferred to Phase 8
  • Missing tables: friends, attendance, reports, notifications
  • Reconsider Google sign-in with hd domain hint vs. separate PFW step
  • Acknowledge the Firebase → Supabase pivot vs. CLAUDE.md

Critical Issues

Critical

The "seeded dev user" approach hides the auth problem instead of solving it

Why it matters
The plan says you'll create a dev user and use it during testing, but it doesn't say how the client authenticates as that user. The three real options are: (a) RLS off, (b) embed a service-role JWT in the app, (c) sign in as a real Supabase Auth user with a known email/password. Options (a) and (b) are both dangerous and produce a giant cliff at Phase 6 — your first real RLS test happens with real users on production data.
Recommendation
Pick option (c) from day 1. Create 2–3 real Supabase Auth users (e.g. dev1@example.com, dev2@example.com) with email/password sign-in enabled. The Expo client signs in once on launch using env-configured credentials. RLS stays on, policies are permissive enough for testing but already shaped like production policies. Phase 6 then becomes "swap the auth provider," not "design and test all RLS policies."
Critical

Gallery moderation cannot be a Phase 8 concern

Why it matters
Phase 4 ships public, all-signed-in-users-can-view photo and video uploads. Phase 8 adds moderation. That window — even at 50 users — is where you get the one inappropriate upload that creates a campus incident. moderation_status exists in the schema but Phase 4 doesn't use it.
Recommendation
From Phase 4 onward: default moderation_status = 'pending' for any anonymous/non-trusted upload, with a simple "auto-approve uploads from creator and from a trusted-user allowlist" rule. Add a media_reports table now (one row per user-flagged piece of media). Document a manual "you can be removed for cause" policy in the app's first content guideline screen. None of this requires ML — it just requires the structure to exist before bad content does.
Critical

Schema is missing tables that the existing UI already implies

Why it matters
The frontend already has a Friends tab, the concept of pinning events to your fridge, and (eventually) notifications. None of these have tables in the plan.
Add
  • friendships(user_a, user_b, status, created_at) — bidirectional pair, normalized so user_a < user_b.
  • event_attendance(event_id, user_id, status: going | interested | dismissed, created_at) — keep "pinned to fridge" distinct from "RSVP'd," because pinning is layout state and attendance is intent.
  • media_reports(media_id, reporter_id, reason, created_at) — see moderation issue above.
  • notifications(user_id, type, payload, read_at, created_at) — at least the table; delivery comes later.
Also add created_at, updated_at, and deleted_at (soft delete) on every user-owned table. The plan implies these but doesn't list them.
Critical

RLS posture is undefined

Why it matters
RLS shows up in the architecture card and in Phase 8, but there's no statement of intent for the intervening phases. In practice, "we'll add RLS at the end" means RLS gets added, breaks every query, and ships under pressure with overly permissive policies because there's no time to audit each one.
Recommendation
Enable RLS on every table at CREATE TABLE time in Phase 0. Start with policies of the form "any authenticated user can read public-by-design tables; only the row owner can write." Tighten as features land. By Phase 6 the policies are already tested with multiple dev users.

Important Issues

Important

Reconsider auth: Google sign-in with the hd hint may collapse two phases into one

Observation
PFW students almost certainly have Google accounts on the pfw.edu hosted domain. Google's OIDC hd claim and the hd auth parameter let you both filter the account chooser and verify the signed-in user is on that workspace. That's stronger than an email-OTP "I have access to a pfw.edu inbox" signal, and it's effectively free.
Implication
Phases 6 and 7 may not need to be separate. "Google sign-in (PFW preferred)" with a soft fallback for non-PFW Google users could replace the entire optional-PFW-OTP step. Keep PFW OTP as a backup for students who use a personal Gmail and still want a verified badge — but that becomes a small convenience feature, not a phase.
Important

The Firebase → Supabase pivot conflicts with CLAUDE.md and isn't acknowledged

Observation
CLAUDE.md documents "Firebase Auth (planned)" as the project's stated direction. The plan silently switches to Supabase. Nothing in the codebase commits to Firebase yet, so the cost of pivoting is essentially zero — but the plan should make the switch explicit and update CLAUDE.md in the same change.
Recommendation
Add a one-paragraph "Why Supabase, not Firebase" decision record (postgres + RLS + storage + auth in one product, less glue code, easier to migrate off later because it's just Postgres). Then update CLAUDE.md so the docs don't drift.
Important

Storage path and bucket policy is unspecified

Why it matters
Supabase Storage RLS is enforced via path patterns. Without a path convention nailed down before Phase 3, the bucket policies become a tangle of regexes.
Recommendation
  • Buckets: flyers (private, signed-URL reads), gallery (public reads after moderation), avatars (public reads).
  • Path convention: {bucket}/{user_id}/{uuid}.{ext} for owned content; gallery/{event_id}/{user_id}/{uuid}.{ext} for event-scoped content.
  • Storage policy: a user can write only under their {user_id} prefix; reads gated by a SQL function that checks moderation_status on the linked gallery_media row.
  • Use expo-image-manipulator for client-side compression before upload (target ~1600px longest edge, JPEG q70 — typical 200–400 KB).
Important

Secret management isn't addressed

Why it matters
The Anthropic API key, Supabase service-role key, Google OAuth client secret, and any future PFW-OTP sender credentials must never ship in the Expo bundle. Expo's extra config is bundled into the app and is publicly readable.
Recommendation
Only the Supabase URL and the anon public key go in the client. Every privileged operation — Anthropic calls, server-issued OTPs, anything using the service-role key — runs in an Edge Function with secrets configured via supabase secrets set. Add a section to the plan that lists which secrets exist, where they live, and which environment (dev/staging/prod) they belong to.
Important

Cost ceiling and storage caps are not quantified

Why it matters
Even at 50 users, gallery videos can blow past the Supabase free tier (1 GB storage) within a single event. AI extraction costs depend on which Claude model and image size you use.
Recommendation
Set explicit per-user limits before Phase 3 ships: e.g. 50 photos per event per user, 10 MB max per upload, video disabled for MVP (or capped at 15 sec / 25 MB). For AI: pick a model (Haiku 4.5 is the right default — cheap, fast, vision-capable) and rate-limit to N scans per user per day. Document the expected monthly $ ceiling so you know when to flip to Supabase Pro.
Important

Fridge layout writes need a debouncing strategy

Why it matters
Personal fridge customization is the "fiddly UX" feature: users will drag, rotate, and re-pin items constantly. A naive "write on every gesture end" hits Supabase dozens of times per minute on a flaky mobile network and creates write contention with the user's other devices.
Recommendation
Hold layout state in memory + AsyncStorage; flush to Supabase on a 1–2 sec debounce, on screen background, and on app close. Use updated_at-based last-write-wins for now (50 users won't hit conflicts often enough to need CRDTs). Make the debounce window configurable so you can tune later.
Important

Indexes aren't called out in the schema plan

Recommendation
Even at 50 users, list these in Phase 0 so they're not retrofit later:
  • events(starts_at) — Market list ordering.
  • events(created_by) — "events I created."
  • fridge_items(user_id) — every fridge load.
  • gallery_media(event_id, created_at) — gallery feed.
  • friendships(user_a), friendships(user_b).

Minor Issues / Worth Considering

Minor

Search strategy on Market

Phase 1 says "search/filter support" but doesn't pick a mechanism. Postgres ILIKE is fine for 50 events. Switch to pg_trgm or full-text search only when titles + descriptions exceed a few thousand rows. Document this so it isn't over-engineered now.

Minor

Realtime is available — decide whether to use it

Supabase Realtime makes "new photos appear in the gallery as friends upload them" very cheap. For 50 users this is well within free-tier limits. It's not required for MVP, but worth a one-line decision rather than finding out about it later.

Minor

Account deletion / GDPR-ish hygiene

Even at 50 users, students do quit campus apps. Plan for a "delete my account and content" path before any real users sign up. Soft-delete on user-owned tables plus a scheduled hard-delete job is enough for MVP.

Minor

Edge Function cold starts for AI extraction

Supabase Edge Functions on Deno have a few hundred ms cold-start penalty after idle. For a one-tap "scan flyer" interaction this is fine if the UI shows a clear loading state, but it should be a deliberate choice rather than a surprise. Mention it in Phase 5.

Minor

Backups

Free-tier Supabase has limited PITR. If real users start uploading data, plan to enable Pro before that point — or take periodic logical dumps via pg_dump. One sentence in Phase 8 is enough; just name it.

Suggested Phase-Ordering Tweaks

Phase Plan as written Suggested change
0 Schema, dev user, "document dev-only features." Same, plus: enable RLS on every table from the start; create 2–3 real Auth users for dev; pick a path/bucket convention; commit indexes.
4 Public galleries with all-signed-in viewing. Add moderation_status default pending at upload, plus media_reports table and an in-app "report" affordance.
6 + 7 Two phases: Google auth, then optional PFW OTP. Likely collapse into one phase using Google's hd=pfw.edu hint as the primary verification signal. Keep OTP only as a fallback for non-PFW Google accounts.
8 RLS, moderation, audit, backups. Trimmed: RLS already on, moderation already structurally in place. Phase 8 becomes "tighten policies, add observability (Sentry / Supabase logs), confirm backups."

Answers to the Plan's Open Questions

  1. Direct Supabase client vs. Edge Functions? For 50 users: direct client + RLS for all CRUD. Edge Functions only for (a) anything that needs a server secret (Anthropic, OAuth exchange, OTP send), (b) operations that require a transactional invariant the client can't be trusted with (e.g. "increment counter and insert log row").
  2. Minimum RLS before beta? Per-row owner-write on every user-owned table; authenticated read on public-by-design tables (events, approved gallery media); no anonymous access at all once dev mode ends. Owner-only read on fridge_items (it's personal layout state).
  3. Moderation before public visibility? Yes — see the critical issue above. pending → approved is required before the upload is readable by other users. "Trusted users" auto-approve on insert via a flag on profiles.
  4. Should PFW verification gate writes? No. PFW verification should be a badge / trust signal that affects auto-approval thresholds and surface ranking, not a hard gate. Otherwise you exclude legitimate students whose primary mail is personal Gmail, and the verification mechanism (mailbox access) doesn't actually prove enrollment anyway.
  5. Storage limits? Per-user: 200 MB total, 10 MB per file, 50 photos per event. Per-event: 2 GB total. Global soft-cap at 15 GB (well below where Supabase Pro becomes mandatory). Revisit when MAU passes 200.

Things the Plan Got Right (worth preserving)

  • "Delay auth in the UX, not in the schema" — this is the single most important sentence in the document.
  • Choosing Supabase over a separate backend service for this scale.
  • Putting AI extraction after manual event creation. This is the right way to de-risk the AI step.
  • Acknowledging that PFW email OTP only proves mailbox access, not enrollment.
  • Storing media paths in Postgres rather than blobs.
  • Separating sticker overlays from the underlying media asset.
Direction is right. The fixes above are about closing operational gaps, not changing the strategy. If the dev-identity, RLS posture, moderation, and missing tables are addressed before Phase 0 is cut, the rest of the plan can ship roughly as-is.
